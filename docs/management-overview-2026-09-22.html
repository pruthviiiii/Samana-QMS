About This Document

This document explains the Samana Queue Management System in business terms. It is written to be read end to end in about twenty minutes, and it assumes no technical knowledge. Where a limit exists, it is stated plainly rather than described as a feature.

One name per thing

Every term in this document matches exactly what appears on screen for staff. Section 01

What the System Does

The system runs the queue at the Customer Experience Centre. It replaces a paper token and a shouted name with four things working together:

PartWhat it is
Check-inA screen at reception, or the customer's own phone after scanning a QR code, where the visit begins.
Staff workspaceThe screen each member of staff uses to see their queue, call a customer, and close the visit.
Reception televisionThe screen in the waiting area showing who is being called and to which counter.
Salesforce linkIdentifies the customer on arrival and writes the finished visit back afterwards.

Between them sits the part nobody sees: the system decides, on its own, which member of staff each customer should go to. That decision is the subject of Workflow B and is where most of the operational value sits.

What it is not

  • It is not an appointment or booking system. It handles people who are already in the building.
  • It is not a CRM. Salesforce remains the customer record; this system reads from it and writes visits back to it.
  • It does not replace judgement. A Manager can always reassign a customer by hand.

Two things that sound alike and are not

TV display is a staff role — the account the waiting-area screen signs in as. The reception television is the physical screen. A sentence such as "give her TV display" means granting an account a role, not handing over a monitor.

Check-in is a customer joining the queue. Sign-in is a member of staff logging into the system. They are different actions with different records behind them.

Two roles worth understanding

Executive is the role that serves customers. It is what the software calls a front-line team member; there is no separate "agent" role.

TV display is an account, not a person. The waiting-area screen signs in with it once and stays signed in. It can show the board and the QR code and can do nothing else — it cannot see customer names or the queue list.

Six services across three departments. The letter is the start of every ticket number for that service, which is how a number on the television identifies what the customer came for.

Naming conflict — needs your decision

Two services are both named General Query: the CRM one (tickets beginning C) and the walk-in one (tickets beginning G). They are different services, handled by different people, and reported separately.

In a report they appear as two rows with the same name, told apart only by a department column that itself reads "General Query". Anyone reading that report will either merge two numbers that should be separate, or ask which is which every time.

Recommendation: rename the walk-in service to something unambiguous — Walk-in Enquiry is the obvious candidate. It is a label change only; ticket numbers, history and reports are unaffected. Listed again in Section 12.

Throughout the rest of this document the two are written as General Query (CRM) and General Query (Walk-in) so that no sentence is ambiguous.

Section 05

Workflow A — A Customer's Visit

The full journey, from the door to the record in Salesforce. Press Play to step through it.

Workflow A · One customer, start to finish
Play walkthrough
1
The customer arrivesCustomer
They either use the check-in screen at reception, or scan the QR code shown on the reception television with their own phone. Both lead to the same place.
2
They are identifiedSystem · Salesforce
They give a mobile number, Emirates ID or passport number. The system asks Salesforce who that is. A customer may type their mobile number in any usual form — with or without 971, with or without a leading zero — and it finds the same person.
3
They choose a serviceCustomer
A registered customer with units picks the unit and one of the CRM services or Collections. Anyone else gets General Query (Walk-in). Nobody can pick a service that does not apply to them.
4
A ticket is issuedSystem
They receive a ticket number — for example C-041 — and, if they checked in by phone, a private link that shows their own progress. Numbers restart each day.
5
The system picks a member of staffSystem
Immediately, not on a timer. The rules are Workflow B. If nobody suitable is free, the ticket waits and stays visible on the board rather than disappearing.
6
The customer is calledExecutive
The member of staff presses Call. The number and counter appear on the reception television straight away, and the customer's phone updates at the same moment.
7
The visit takes placeExecutive
They press Start when the customer arrives at the counter. The ticket moves from the announcement to the board, now showing the counter number.
8
The visit is closedExecutive
They press Close and may add a note. If the customer never came forward, they press No-show instead. Either way the outcome is recorded against the ticket.
9
The next customer is offered at onceSystem
Closing a visit immediately offers that member of staff the next customer waiting for a service they cover. They are not left idle.
10
The visit is written backSystem · Salesforce
Within about fifteen seconds the completed visit, including any note, is sent to Salesforce. If Salesforce is unavailable it is retried automatically, and flagged for attention if it keeps failing.
Steps 1–5 take seconds. Step 6 is the only point at which a person waits for another person.
Section 06

Workflow B — How a Customer Reaches a Member of Staff

This is the part that used to be a supervisor walking the floor. The rules are applied in order; the first one that fits decides.

Workflow B · The routing rules, in order
Play walkthrough
1
Does this customer already have a relationship manager?
If Salesforce names an owner for the unit and that person is signed in, covering the service, and not already with a customer, they get it. The relationship is honoured first.
2
Is that person mid-visit?
They may hold one customer waiting for them, for up to five minutes. A second customer for the same person goes to the rotation instead, so a queue never builds on one desk.
3
Is this Collections?
Collections goes to the unit's collection manager rather than by rotation. If no manager is mapped, the ticket is shown as needing attention rather than quietly left unassigned.
4
Otherwise, whose turn is it?
Among everyone signed in, covering that service, and not already holding a customer, the system picks whoever has waited longest for work — counting work at every counter they cover, not just this one.
5
Nobody available?
The ticket stays on the board marked as waiting for a member of staff. It is not hidden, and it is picked up the moment somebody becomes free.

6.1 Three rules worth knowing

RuleWhy it is there
One customer at a timeNobody is given a second customer while they still hold one. Previously ten waiting customers could all carry one person's name while nobody else could pick them up, and the board read as though one person was serving ten. Closing a visit offers the next customer immediately, so this costs no waiting time.
Fair turns across countersSomeone covering three services is measured on all their work, not on each counter separately. Before this, whoever helped in the most places was handed the most customers — helping more meant working more.
Newcomers join the backAdding somebody to a service puts them at the end of the rotation, not the front. Previously they jumped ahead of everyone who had been working that queue all day.

6.2 Urgency between services

Each service carries an urgency: Normal, Elevated, High or Urgent. A customer waiting for a higher-urgency service is routed before an older customer waiting elsewhere — so a Refund can be put ahead of a General Query if that is the policy.

Urgency never reorders one queue against itself

Inside a single service, arrival order always decides. Urgency only affects which service is served first when people are waiting in several. This is deliberate: a customer who can see somebody who arrived after them being called first would rightly object, and no setting should be able to cause that.

All six services are currently set to Normal. Changing that is a management decision — see Section 12.

Section 07

Workflow C — A Working Day

WhenWhoWhat happens
OpeningReceptionThe reception television is switched on and signs in once with the TV display account. It shows the QR code and the board. If it has been off for a long time it may need signing in again — see Section 08.
OpeningEvery ExecutiveEach signs in and sets themselves online, choosing their counter. Until somebody is online, no customer can be assigned to them.
During the dayCustomersCheck in at reception or by phone. Tickets are issued and assigned immediately.
During the daySystemEvery fifteen seconds it reconsiders anything waiting — picking up customers nobody took, moving on from staff who have disappeared, and sending completed visits to Salesforce.
BreaksExecutiveGoing offline stops new customers arriving. The system will not let somebody go offline while they still have a customer at their counter.
ClosingExecutiveClose or mark no-show on anything remaining, then go offline.
OvernightSystemAnything still waiting from a previous day is marked no-show automatically once it is more than two hours past the day boundary, so the morning starts clean. A visit still in progress is left for its Executive to finish.
Next morningSystemTicket numbers restart. A number still showing on the floor from the previous day is skipped, so two live tickets never share a number.
Being signed in is not the same as being online

A member of staff can be signed in and still be offline — on a break, or between counters. Only online staff receive customers. If nobody in a service is online, customers for that service wait and the board shows them as waiting for a member of staff.

The system notices a screen that has died — a closed laptop or lost connection — within about forty-five seconds, marks that person offline, and moves their waiting customer to somebody else on the next pass.

Section 08

Workflow D — When Things Fail

Every failure below degrades the service rather than stopping it. What matters operationally is the last column: who finds out, and how.

If this happensWhat customers and staff seeHow it is noticed
Salesforce is slow or downCustomers cannot be looked up, but reception can still issue a ticket as a walk-in. Nobody is turned away.Flagged on the system health page. Those visits are marked so they can be reconciled later.
A member of staff's screen diesThey stop receiving customers after about forty-five seconds; anyone waiting for them is moved on.They appear offline on the team screen.
The reception television's session expiresThe screen says its sign-in has expired and asks somebody to sign in again.Visible on the screen itself. It no longer blames the network and wait silently, which was the previous behaviour.
Two Executives call the same customerThe second is told the ticket was just updated and their screen refreshes. The customer is served once.Self-correcting; no action needed.
Nobody covers a serviceCustomers for it wait, shown on the board as waiting for a member of staff.Removing the last person from a service asks for confirmation first.
The routing service stopsCheck-in and manual calling keep working. Nothing is reassigned and no visit reaches Salesforce.A banner appears in the staff workspace, and the system health page reports a fault.
A visit will not reach SalesforceNo visible effect on the floor.Retried automatically with increasing delays, then flagged for a person after five attempts.
Nobody is currently told automatically

The system has a single health page that reports, in one place, whenever any of the above needs a person. It works — during a recent fault it reported the problem correctly for the whole duration.

Nothing is watching that page. Faults are currently found by somebody noticing. Connecting an automatic monitor to it is a small, external task and is the single most valuable operational improvement outstanding. It needs an owner and an escalation contact, which is a management decision — see Section 12.

One field deserves attention

A person's Salesforce ID is typed in by hand when their account is created. It is what causes a customer's own relationship manager to receive them. If it is mistyped, that person's customers are simply never routed to them — silently, with no error to anyone. There is a search tool to find the right value, and it is worth confirming for every Executive during setup.

Section 10

What Is Recorded

Every action is recorded with who did it and when. This is what lets a question about a past visit be answered from the record rather than from memory.

RecordedQuestion it answers
Ticket issuedWhen did this customer arrive, and for what?
Assigned, with the reasonWhy did this customer go to this person and not another?
Called, started, closed or no-showHow long did they wait, and how long did the visit take?
ReassignedWho moved this customer, and when?
Customer lookupWho searched for a customer, and was Salesforce available?
Sign-in, failed sign-in, sign-outWas this account in use at the time?
Going online and offlineWas anybody actually available at 11am?
Staff and queue changesWho changed this person's services or role?
Report exportsWho exported customer data, when, and with what filters?

Reporting available on screen

  • Tickets by day, service and department, with completion counts.
  • Average waiting time and average service time.
  • The full activity log, searchable by action and date range, with paging.
  • Export to spreadsheet for any date range up to one year.
One limit worth stating

Sign-in is recorded, but not the device or network address it came from. If an account were misused, the record shows when and what it did, but not from where. Adding that is small work and is listed in Section 12.

Section 11

Personal Data

The system holds customer names, mobile numbers, Emirates ID and passport numbers, and unit details. How that is handled:

ControlHow it works
ID numbers are not shown casuallyThey appear on a ticket's detail for the staff serving that customer, and nowhere else on the floor.
Export is deliberate and recordedID numbers leave the system only when a Manager explicitly asks for them on an export, and that request is written to the activity log with its filters and who made it.
The reception television shows numbers onlyThe waiting area sees ticket numbers, services and counters. No names.
A customer's phone shows only their own visitTheir ticket number and how many people are ahead of them. No other customer's ticket number appears, deliberately.
Old records are clearedIdentifiers can be anonymised and old activity removed automatically after a set period. The period is a management decision — see Section 12.
Access follows the roleAn Executive sees their own customers. Reception sees the queue but cannot serve. The television account sees neither.
Where the weakness is

Access is granted by an Administrator and stays until an Administrator removes it. There is no single sign-on, no two-factor authentication, and no link to HR — so a leaver keeps access until somebody remembers to switch it off.

For a system holding identity documents, that is the most significant remaining gap. It is a policy and investment decision rather than a defect, and it should be the first item of any next phase.

Section 12

Decisions Required

Eight items that need a decision from management rather than work from engineering. Ordered by what would cause the most trouble if left.

#DecisionEffortWhy it matters
1Who is alerted when the system reports a fault, and howSmall, externalThe health page works and nothing is watching it. Until an alert reaches a person, faults are found by someone noticing. Needs a named owner and a contact method.
2Rename the walk-in General Query serviceSmallTwo services share a name and appear as two identically-labelled rows in reports. Walk-in Enquiry is suggested. Label only; no history is affected.
3Acceptance testing on the real television, printer and phonesHalf a dayThe screens have been tested in software but never on the actual hardware in the Centre. Layout, sound and printing need eyes on the real devices before launch.
4Urgency policy per serviceMinutesAll six are Normal. If Refunds or Collections should be served ahead of general enquiries, that is now a setting rather than a development request.
5How long customer identifiers are keptMinutesAutomatic clearing of ID numbers and old activity is built but switched off pending a retention period from the business.
6Single sign-on, two-factor, and leaver removalProjectThe largest remaining security gap (Section 11). Needs a decision on direction and budget.
7SMS to customers on check-inSmall, externalThe system can text a customer their ticket, but no SMS provider is connected. Needs a provider and an account.
8One customer has two Salesforce accountsSalesforce teamFound during testing: one phone number appears on two records, stored in different formats. The system now always picks the same one, but the duplicate should be merged at source.
What is not on this list

Nothing here blocks the system from running. Items 1 and 3 are the two that should be closed before the Centre relies on it in earnest: one so faults reach a person, the other so nobody discovers a layout problem on the television with a waiting room full of customers.
